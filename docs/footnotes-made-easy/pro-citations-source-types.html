Citations — Source Types Pro

Footnotes Made Easy Pro › Citations Edit this page

Footnotes Made Easy Pro supports 10 source types. Each source type has a specific set of fields that map to the formatting rules for APA, MLA, and Chicago citation styles.

Supported source types

1. Book

Use for physical or digital books, including edited volumes and multi-volume works.

Fields:

  • Author(s)
  • Title
  • Edition (optional)
  • Publisher
  • Year of publication
  • City of publication (Chicago only)
  • ISBN (optional — used for auto-fetch)

Example (APA):

Smith, J. A. (2021). The Art of Writing. Oxford University Press.

2. Journal Article

Use for articles published in academic or professional journals.

Fields:

  • Author(s)
  • Article title
  • Journal name
  • Volume
  • Issue (optional)
  • Page range
  • Year of publication
  • DOI (optional — used for auto-fetch)
  • URL (optional)

Example (APA):

Brown, L., & Jones, M. (2022). Climate patterns in sub-Saharan Africa. Journal of Environmental Science, 45(3), 112–128. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx

3. Website

Use for web pages, online articles, and digital content without a formal publication structure.

Fields:

  • Author(s) or organisation
  • Page title
  • Website name
  • Publication or last updated date
  • URL
  • Access date (MLA and Chicago)

Example (MLA):

Garcia, Maria. "The Future of Renewable Energy." Energy Today, 14 Mar. 2023, www.energytoday.com/future-renewable-energy.

4. Newspaper Article

Use for articles published in print or online newspapers.

Fields:

  • Author(s)
  • Article title
  • Newspaper name
  • Publication date
  • Page or section (optional)
  • URL (optional)

Example (Chicago):

Williams, Robert. "New Policy Shifts Housing Market." The Daily Tribune, March 5, 2023.

5. Magazine Article

Use for articles published in magazines or general-interest periodicals.

Fields:

  • Author(s)
  • Article title
  • Magazine name
  • Publication date
  • Page range (optional)
  • URL (optional)

6. Film or Video

Use for films, documentaries, television episodes, and online video content.

Fields:

  • Title
  • Director
  • Producer or production company
  • Year of release
  • Format or platform (e.g. Netflix, theatrical release)
  • URL (optional, for online video)

Example (MLA):

Inception. Directed by Christopher Nolan, Warner Bros. Pictures, 2010.

7. Thesis or Dissertation

Use for master's theses, doctoral dissertations, and similar academic works.

Fields:

  • Author
  • Title
  • Degree type (e.g. PhD dissertation, Master's thesis)
  • Institution
  • Year
  • Database or URL (optional)

Example (APA):

Thompson, K. R. (2020). Digital identity formation in adolescents [Doctoral dissertation, University of Chicago].

8. Book Chapter

Use for chapters or essays in edited volumes or anthologies.

Fields:

  • Chapter author(s)
  • Chapter title
  • Editor(s)
  • Book title
  • Page range
  • Publisher
  • Year of publication

Example (Chicago):

Adams, Sarah. "Colonial Narratives in Modern Fiction." In Postcolonial Perspectives, edited by James Okafor, 45–72. Cambridge University Press, 2019.

9. Conference Paper

Use for papers presented at academic conferences, whether published in proceedings or not.

Fields:

  • Author(s)
  • Paper title
  • Conference name
  • Conference date
  • Conference location
  • Publisher or proceedings title (if published)
  • Page range (if published)
  • URL (optional)

10. Other / Generic

Use for source types not covered by the above categories — government reports, legal documents, social media posts, interviews, podcasts, and similar sources.

Fields:

  • Author or creator
  • Title
  • Source type description
  • Publication or creation date
  • Publisher or platform
  • URL (optional)
  • Additional notes

The Other type gives you a free-form description field to capture source details that do not fit a standard structure. The citation is formatted as closely as possible to the selected style guide's guidance for non-standard sources.

Adding a source type not listed here

If you regularly cite a source type not covered above, please submit a feature request via our support page. We review feature requests for each product update.
