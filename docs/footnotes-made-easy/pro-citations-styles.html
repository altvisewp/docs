Citation Styles Pro

Footnotes Made Easy Pro › Citations Edit this page

Footnotes Made Easy Pro supports three major academic citation styles. Each style has its own formatting conventions for author names, punctuation, italics, and field ordering.

APA (7th Edition)

The American Psychological Association style is used widely in social sciences, psychology, education, nursing, and business.

General format

In-text: Superscript number linking to the reference entry.

Reference list format:

Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of work: Capital letter also for subtitle. Publisher. https://doi.org/xxxxx

Key conventions

  • Author names use last name followed by initials: Smith, J. A.
  • Multiple authors separated by & before the last author
  • Year of publication in parentheses immediately after author names
  • Book and journal titles in italics
  • Article titles in sentence case (only first word and proper nouns capitalised)
  • Journal names in title case and italics
  • DOI formatted as a URL: https://doi.org/xxxxx
  • No access dates for stable online content; access dates for content likely to change

Examples

Book:

Grohol, J. M. (2020). Essentials of online counselling. American Counselling Association.

Journal article:

Kim, S., & Park, J. H. (2021). Social media use and adolescent mental health. Journal of Adolescent Health, 68(4), 245–252. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jadohealth.2020.09.001

Website:

World Health Organization. (2023, January 12). Mental health. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/mental-health-strengthening-our-response

MLA (9th Edition)

The Modern Language Association style is used widely in humanities disciplines — literature, language studies, film, and cultural studies.

General format

In-text: Superscript number linking to the works cited entry.

Works cited format:

Author Last, First. "Title of Source." Container Title, Other Contributors, Version, Number, Publisher, Date, Location.

Key conventions

  • Author names use last name first for the first author: Smith, John
  • Subsequent authors use normal order: and Jane Doe
  • Source titles in quotation marks; container titles (journals, websites, books containing the source) in italics
  • All major words capitalised in titles
  • Date of access included for web sources
  • URLs or DOIs optional but recommended for online sources
  • Period at the end of each entry

Examples

Book:

Morrison, Toni. Beloved. Vintage Books, 1987.

Journal article:

Patel, Rina, and David Chen. "Urban Green Spaces and Mental Wellbeing." Urban Studies Quarterly, vol. 14, no. 2, 2022, pp. 88–104.

Website:

"Climate Change Evidence." NASA, 10 Feb. 2023, climate.nasa.gov/evidence/.

Chicago (17th Edition)

The Chicago Manual of Style is used widely in history, arts, and some social sciences. It supports two systems — Notes-Bibliography (NB) and Author-Date. Footnotes Made Easy Pro uses the Notes-Bibliography system, which is the more common choice for humanities writing.

General format

Footnote/endnote:

First M. Last, Title of Book (Place of Publication: Publisher, Year), page number.

Bibliography entry:

Last, First M. Title of Book. Place of Publication: Publisher, Year.

Key conventions

  • Footnote entries are formatted differently from bibliography entries
  • First citations are written in full; subsequent citations of the same source use a shortened form
  • Author full name in footnotes; last name first in bibliography
  • Place of publication included for books
  • Page numbers in footnotes for specific references
  • Titles of major works in italics; titles of shorter works in quotation marks
  • Ibid. may be used for consecutive citations of the same source

Examples

Book footnote:

1. Sarah Johnson, The Industrial Revolution Reconsidered (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2018), 45.

Shortened form (subsequent citation):

4. Johnson, Industrial Revolution, 112.

Journal article footnote:

2. Marcus Webb and Li Huang, "Trade Routes in Medieval Asia," Journal of Historical Geography 38, no. 2 (2020): 201.

Website footnote:

3. "About the Archive," Internet Archive, last modified January 5, 2023, https://archive.org/about/.

Choosing a citation style

If you are unsure which style to use:

  • Academic writing in social sciences, psychology, or education → APA
  • Academic writing in literature, film, or humanities → MLA
  • Academic writing in history or arts → Chicago
  • General non-academic writing → any style works; choose the one most familiar to your readers

For formal academic submissions (journals, theses, dissertations), always verify the required style with your institution or publisher. Footnotes Made Easy Pro follows current edition guidelines but formal submissions should be reviewed against the official style manual.
